1. Project Title

The Unstable Icon: Semiotic Velocity in the 160-Year Ontogeny of Jan Matejko's Stańczyk (1862–Present).

 2. Main Objective and Summary of the Project

The primary goal is to theorize and measure "Semiotic Velocity," defined as the rate at which a visual sign's dominant meaning shifts across temporal and political contexts. 

The project utilizes a purpose-built Semiotic Velocity Instrument (archived at github.com/esltogo20-ship-it/semiotic-velocity-tool). This tool employs a Dual-Stream Pipeline—integrating visual hashing and linguistic Natural Language Processing (NLP)—to generate quantitative evidence of semantic mutation. 

A core focus is the "2010 Digital Explosion," using it as a diagnostic threshold to measure how mobile connectivity and social media proliferation accelerated the visual instability of Jan Matejko's Stańczyk. This provides a robust proof of concept (POC) for the velocity metric.

 3. Background to the Project and Relevance

Cultural semiotics confirms sign instability but currently lacks a framework for comparing why some meanings transform rapidly while others persist. This project introduces Semiotic Velocity as a measurable variable within Lotmanian semiotics and proposes "Visual Aesopian Language"—an adaptation of Lev Loseff’s literary theory to image. This describes the capacity of an artifact to maintain plausible innocence while conveying politically dangerous meanings.

The project is relevant to the Faculty as it bridges the "logical fracture" between visual perception and persuasive intent, specifically addressing how the digital landscape since 2010 has lowered the friction of iconographic recontextualization. This aligns with current research in Multimodal Digital Humanities and Slavonic Studies by providing a computational bridge to traditional art-historical analysis.

 4. Theoretical Framework

The framework is grounded in Juri Lotman’s cultural semiotics (the invariant core and the semiosphere). This is coupled with the Derrida-Laclau spectrum as a political valence axis to track movement between constitutive instability and the empty signifier. Furthermore, Aby Warburg’s "Pathosformel" is used to identify the "Sad Clown Paradox" as the engine driving the sign’s international velocity. This foundation was chosen to provide a rigorous, interdisciplinary balance between structural coherence and post-structuralist deconstruction.

 5. Research Questions and Expected Findings (Hypotheses)
Question 1: How does the disruption of authorized institutional carriers correlate with the rate of semiotic mutation?. Findings will likely demonstrate that velocity peaks during periods of "Institutional Rupture" (e.g., 1939, 1956, 1989).

Question 2: To what extent does new reproductive technology lower the friction of derivative production?. It is expected that each technological shift (lithograph to digital meme) accelerates new semantic attachments.

Question 3: Does the jester function as a "cultural pronoun" for ideological occupancy?. Evidence suggests the image is structurally encoded for contradictory occupancy by opposing political factions.

 6. Methods and Research Ethics
The Semiotic Velocity Instrument is operational and hosted on GitHub. The methodology utilizes a Python-based pipeline:

Visual Stream: Employs perceptual hashing (pHash) to cluster visual derivatives of Stańczyk across disparate digital archives[cite: 1].

Linguistic Stream: Uses NLP to capture nominal deployments and sentiment shifts associated with the image[cite: 1].

Multimodal Intent Reasoning (AD-MIR): A novel analytic layer that uses Spatio-Temporal Grid Projection to align pixel-level clustering with qualitative persuasive intent[cite: 1].

The project adheres to ethical standards regarding the use of public-domain digital artifacts and prioritizes the protection of user metadata when scraping social media environments, ensuring compliance with current Digital Humanities research protocols.
